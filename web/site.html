Digital communications over FM radio

FM Multi-Mod Link

Built by Radithya Arsa Wibowo · Fundamentals of Communication Systems, PENS (Surabaya, Indonesia)

An Arduino Uno turns digital data into audio tones with ASK, FSK or DBPSK, an FM transmitter puts them on the air, an FM receiver picks them up again, and a laptop demodulates them and checks the CRC. This page walks through every stage and can decode the signal itself.

  • FM transmitter on the air, around 102–104 MHz
  • End-to-end link works for FSK, ASK and DBPSK
  • RDA5807M receiver: the new module picks up the transmitter (RSSI 107 of 127)
  • RDA audio to the laptop not wired yet; decoding still goes through a phone FM radio

    01 · Data

    Frame: the envelope around every message

    The receiver doesn't know when a message starts, so every message is wrapped: a preamble to lock onto the bit timing, a sync word to mark the start, the data length, the payload, and a CRC that tells whether any bit was corrupted. Type below and watch the frame change.

    preamblesynctype + len payloadCRC-8idle
    bits sent (MSB first), filled box = 1
    preamble · AA AA AA AA32 bits of 1010… so the receiver can lock onto the bit timing.
    sync · 2D D416-bit sync word. The decoder looks for the last 8 preamble bits plus this word (24 bits, one error allowed).
    type · 3C / C33C = text, C3 = PRBS. They differ in all 8 bits, so the type still reads correctly with 3 bit errors.
    lenNumber of payload bytes, 0–100 (the Uno has only 2 KB of RAM).
    payloadThe message: Latin-1 text, or 64 bytes of PRBS-9 for measuring the BER.
    CRC-8 · poly 0x07Computed over type, len and payload. If the received CRC doesn't match the computed one, the frame is dropped.
    idle · FF FF FF FF32 bits of gap between frames.

    02 · Modulation

    One bit, three ways to make it audible

    Bits can't go over an FM radio as they are, because FM carries sound. So every bit becomes an audio tone. The waveform below uses exactly the samples the Uno makes (31 250 Hz DDS, 8-bit sine table).

    ASK · Amplitude Shift Keying

    1 → 1800 Hz tone · 0 → silence

    The simplest: the tone switches on and off. The weakness: noise and volume changes can be mistaken for a 1.

    FSK · Frequency Shift Keying

    1 → 1200 Hz · 0 → 2200 Hz

    There is always a tone; only its frequency changes, and the phase stays continuous. The decoder compares the energy at 1200 and 2200 Hz, so loudness doesn't matter.

    DBPSK · Differential BPSK

    0 → phase flipped 180° · 1 → phase kept

    A steady 1800 Hz tone whose phase changes. The decoder compares each bit with the one before, so it needs no phase reference.

    03 · Inside the Arduino Uno

    DDS, PWM, then an RC filter

    The Uno has no DAC. So Timer1 fires an interrupt 31 250 times a second. Each time, a 32-bit phase accumulator advances, its top 8 bits index a sine table, and the result becomes the duty cycle of a 62.5 kHz PWM on pin D3. A two-stage RC filter then smooths the PWM pulses into an audio wave.

    DDS calculator

    1200 Hz
    DDS samples → PWM pulses → filter output (1.28 ms)

    RC filter response + de-emphasis

    R1 = R2 = 1.5 kΩ, C1 = C2 = 10 nF, loaded by a 10 kΩ trimmer. Each stage alone has fc = 10.6 kHz, but the two stages load each other, so together they are −3 dB at about 5 kHz. The FM receiver adds 50 µs de-emphasis.

    04 · FM transmitter

    The audio tone pushes the carrier frequency around

    An S-151 kit transmitter (C828 oscillator + C930 amplifier, 9 V battery) takes the Uno's audio through a 3.5 mm plug. The larger the audio voltage, the further the carrier frequency moves. This shift, the deviation, is set with a 10 kΩ trimmer.

    The oscillator is a simple LC one, so the frequency moves when the battery drops or a hand comes close. The Find transmitter button in the live demo makes the RDA5807M search ±1.5 MHz for it.

    Too much trimmer gives too much deviation and distorted sound; too little buries the signal in hiss. Experiment E7 measures exactly that.

    06 · Receiver

    Catching the FM signal again

    The main receiver is an RDA5807M chip controlled by the Uno over I2C. The first module turned out to have a dead RF front end; the replacement (26 Nov) picks up the transmitter at RSSI 107. Its audio isn't wired to the laptop yet, so text decoding still uses a phone FM radio.

    RDA5807M (RRD-102 module)

    Single-chip FM receiver, 76–108 MHz, 3.3 V. The 5 V Uno talks to it through a BSS138 level converter. The Uno sets the frequency, volume and mono mode, and reads the RSSI (signal strength, 0–127).

    I2C address
    0x10, 0x11 (+0x60)
    Tuning
    reg 03 = (ch << 6) | 0x10
    RSSI
    reg 0B >> 9

    Phone FM radio

    Earphones plugged in as the antenna, sound out of the speaker, picked up by the laptop microphone. Used for decoding and for a demo you can hear. Too rough for formal measurements, but enough to prove the link works end to end.

    RTL-SDR + SDR++ (planned)

    Once an RTL-SDR arrives, the transmitter's spectrum and drift show up on a waterfall, and the demodulated audio can be decoded without any cable.

    Diagnosing module 1: raw registers from the REG command

    The first module answered on I2C but did not pick up a single station, even with a 75 cm antenna. Its registers were read directly to separate firmware problems from hardware problems.

    RegisterValueMeaning
    005804genuine RDA5807M chip ID
    02E005audio on, not muted, mono, chip enabled
    032A40channel 169 → 87.0 + 16.9 = 103.9 MHz
    0A40A9tuning done (STC = 1) on channel 169
    0B048FRSSI = 2, FM_TRUE = 0: no signal coming in

    Conclusion: firmware and I2C are fine, but this module's RF section is dead. With the transmitter 10–30 cm away it read only about RSSI 10, where a healthy module reads 50 or more. The replacement module (26 Nov) reads 107–108 at the transmitter's frequency and 28–55 elsewhere.

    07 · Send and decode

    Send from the laptop, decode in the browser

    The Transmitter panel sends text to the Uno over USB, and the Uno broadcasts it over FM. The RDA receiver panel shows the RDA5807M picking up that transmitter, with a live signal strength.     How to run the demo with the transmitter and a phone radio
    1. Plug the transmitter's audio cable into the jack on the breadboard, switch the transmitter on, and keep it about 1 m from the Uno.
    2. In the Transmitter panel: click Connect Uno, pick the Arduino port (usually COM3), type a text, choose the scheme and bit rate, then Send to Uno. Without Chrome or Edge, the same commands can be typed in the Arduino IDE Serial Monitor: M halo PDSK, S0 / S1 / S2, R300.
    3. In the RDA receiver panel: click Find transmitter. The RDA tunes to the strongest frequency, and the signal bar rises when the transmitter is on and drops when it is off.
    4. Tune a phone FM radio to the transmitter until the data sound is clear, and switch its speaker on. If the station is hard to find, click 1000 Hz tone first, then Back to data.
    5. Hold the phone speaker near the laptop microphone, press Microphone, and allow microphone access.
    6. Frames that come through appear in the table. If they all fail, check the radio frequency (the transmitter drifts) and turn the phone volume down if the sound distorts.
